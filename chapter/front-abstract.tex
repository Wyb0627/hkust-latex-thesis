Language models utilize external information to make predictions and answer questions when their inputs lack the required knowledge. Explicit knowledge structures can expose relationships that help select and use this information. However, augmentation with an existing knowledge graph depends on its coverage, and supervised model adaptation requires labeled data and fine-tuning. This thesis investigates structural augmentation from knowledge-enriched representation learning to task-related graph retrieval and reasoning.

We first propose KGLink to enrich column representations with knowledge-graph descriptions and learn their correspondence to annotation labels. To remove the dependence on a predefined knowledge graph and task-specific fine-tuning, we propose GORAG to construct and update a task graph for dynamic few-shot classification. Connected retrieval supplies candidate labels to a large language model. We then extend retrieval beyond candidate labels to facts that support an answer. AGRAG selects connected textual facts and supporting passages for question answering and generation. MGRAG constructs query-specific multimodal graphs, matches query relations to semantic paths, and uses graph structure to guide the reuse and recomputation of cached model states.

Experiments across these tasks show improvements in prediction and answer quality, with efficiency gains in graph indexing and context processing. Ablation studies examine the contributions of the structural components. A related study of hyper-relational knowledge graphs analyzes information preservation under graph transformations.
