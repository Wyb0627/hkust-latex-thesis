R3O2-5: In the graph retrieval step, it is unclear why “clean-
ing” of raw mapped triples is necessary. These triples origi-
nate from the KG already constructed earlier in the pipeline
and should presumably be clean. By cleaning the triples, did
the authors mean retrieving the most relevant facts?
Reply to R3O2-5: Thanks for your insightful suggestions.
The triples in KG may not be “clean” enough with respect
to the given triple, as in the KG construction step, the triple
extraction is not query awared. In the graph retrieval step, the
triple selection is based on their sematic embedding similarity
with the query. There might be some semantically similar, but
not query relevant triples selected, we apply the LLM fact
triple filter w.r.t. the given query, to further strengthen the co-
relevance between between the final mapped triplet facts and
the query. We have revise the related part in Section III-C
to clear the motivation of the triplet cleaning step (Equation
(9)). Also, we add a table compare AGRAG’s performance
on Novel and Medical datasets with and without the filter of
raw mapped triples in R-Table VI and VII. According to R-
Table VI, VII and Table VI, the triplet “cleaning” procedure
incurs only a slight increase in time but can improve model
effectiveness; we consider it worthwhile.

                              V. C ONCLUSION
   This paper presents AGRAG, an Advanced Graph-based
Retrieval Augmented Generation framework addressing the In-
accurate Graph Construction, Poor Reasoning Ability, and In-
adequate Answer issues of current RAG frameworks. AGRAG
introduces a statistics-based entity extraction method to re-
place the LLM entity extraction method applied by current
baselines, avoiding hallucination, improving effectiveness, and
having better efficiency. By formulating retrieval as a Mini-
mum Cost Maximum Influence (MCMI) subgraph generation
problem, AGRAG captures further query-relevant semantic
dependencies by allowing more complex graph structure, and
the reasoning paths can be more comprehensive and include
more query-related graph nodes. By providing explicit reason-
ing paths to help LLM inference, AGRAG makes the LLM
better focus on query-related contexts within the retrieved
context. These can lead to better performance on complex
tasks that require content summarization. Experiments on real-
world datasets demonstrate the effectveness and efficiency of
AGRAG over state-of-the-art RAG models.
