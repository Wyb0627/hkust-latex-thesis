8.2.3 Joint retrieval and context computation
A third direction is to study retrieval and context serving as coupled decisions. Retrieved ev-
idence determines the length, modality, and structural organization of the model’s input. The
available cached states and the recomputation policy then determine the cost of processing that
input. MGRAG provides one point in this design space by using graph features in selective
recomputation.
Further work could evaluate how alternative evidence structures affect the quality–latency
tradeoff under a fixed serving budget. The comparison would need to separate graph construc-
tion,matching,cachereuse,andanswergeneration,andretainthequeryworkloadandhardware
conditions. This would connect the semantic value of retrieved evidence to the cost of using
it, extending the thesis’s focus on structure from context selection to the complete evidence-
processingpath.
