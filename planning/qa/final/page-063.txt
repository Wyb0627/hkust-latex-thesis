4.5.1 Compared methods and controls
The study compares six baselines. Entailment constructs text–label entailment pairs and fine-
tunes a classifier [36]. NaiveRAG retrieves text chunks from the indexed support information.
Retrieved context is compressed by LongLLMLingua before classification [41]. GraphRAG,
LightRAG, and HippoRAG2 supply graph-based retrieval alternatives [17, 19, 42]. Their clas-
sificationresultsaremeasurementsoftheadaptationsusedinthisstudy.
EntailmentusesRoBERTa-largeforfivetrainingepochs,abatchsizeof16,alearningrateof
10−6, and Adam. LongLLMLingua is evaluated with compression rates of 0.75, 0.8, and 0.85,
with 0.8 selected in the reported comparison. GraphRAG and LightRAG use local search. The
source uses the nano-graphrag implementation for GraphRAG and NaiveRAG and the other
baselines’ oﬀicial implementations. These choices identify the systems that produced the re-
portedresults.
The baseline collection tests several ways of allocating external context. NaiveRAG selects
passages. LongLLMLingua changes which tokens remain in that context. The graph base-
lines select graph-associated information. GORAG explicitly selects labels through a connec-
tion structure and expands its lexical index online. These differences motivate the comparison,
whiletheablationsisolatechangeswithinGORAG’sownpipelinemoredirectly.
