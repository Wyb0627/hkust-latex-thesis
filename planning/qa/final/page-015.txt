1.1 Motivation
Language models use tables, text, and images to make predictions and generate answers. The
required information is often incomplete or distributed across sources. A table may list people
without identifying their occupations. A classification task may introduce labels absent from
its initial training examples. A question may require facts from several passages or a relation-
ship expressed jointly by text and images. Selecting the relevant evidence and preserving its
connectionsarethereforepartofthepredictionproblem.
We study how explicit knowledge structures can support evidence selection for language
models. Knowledge graphs provide descriptions absent from a table. Task graphs maintain as-
sociations between keywords and classification labels. Evidence graphs connect facts across
passages, while query graphs specify the relationships needed to answer a multimodal ques-
tion. Wedevelopmethodsthatusetheserelationshipstoretrieveevidenceandcontrolhowitis
processed.
