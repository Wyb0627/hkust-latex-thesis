Figure 2.1 reproduces the original comparison, including database reification. The study
distinguishes main-triple information, intra-qualifier information, and inter-qualifier informa-
tion. A decomposition can retain qualifier entities and relation labels while changing how their
associationwiththemainfactisexpressed. Databasereificationintroducesauxiliaryentitiesand
relations, also changing the original main-triple topology. These differences determine which
neighborhoodsandpathsanordinaryKGEmodelreceivesafterconversion.
The association of a qualifier with a statement is especially relevant when a subject partici-
pates in several facts using the same relation. In the education example, a degree describes one
university attendance statement. A collection of subject–degree and subject–university triples
exposes the entities but changes the representation of which degree qualifies which attendance
fact. A model following ordinary graph paths then receives the transformed connections. The
Hyper method supplies additional connections intended to retain more of these associations,
whereas a qualifier integrator receives the main fact and its qualifier pairs together. This com-
parisonplacesinformationpreservationattheinterfacebetweenthedatarepresentationandthe
computation that follows it. It concerns the semantic organization of the input as well as its
vocabulary.
