The node score is computed before neighborhood expansion. Consequently, a candidate’s
influence refers to its position in the indexed graph under the query anchors. The subgraph

This distinction separates propagation over the evidence source from expansion of the context
returned to the model. The former establishes a score landscape; the latter selects an evidence
structureusingthatlandscapeandthesemanticedgecosts.
Influence and direct similarity answer different retrieval questions. Similarity asks how
closely a triple’s wording corresponds to the query. Influence additionally depends on graph
connectivity to query anchors. A node can receive influence through connections to several
mapped facts. The subgraph selection stage uses these scores alongside edge costs so that the
retrievaldecisionreflectsbothkindsofinformation.
