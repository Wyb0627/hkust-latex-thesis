Abstract
Language models depend on the evidence supplied to their prediction and generation pro-
cesses. Tables can omit information needed for semantic annotation, classification labels can
evolve under limited supervision, and answers can require relationships distributed across tex-
tualandvisualsources. Thisthesisinvestigateshowexplicitknowledgestructurescanorganize
andselectevidenceforthesesettings.
Thethesisdevelopsfourmethodsforstructuralaugmentation. KGLinkintegratesknowledge
graphs with a pre-trained language model to enrich column representations and bridge differ-
ences between graph types and annotation labels. GORAG supports dynamic few-shot classi-
fication through connected label retrieval and an online graph index that evolves with the task.
AGRAG selects connected textual evidence by combining query-dependent node influence, se-
manticedgecosts,andhybridpassageretrieval. MGRAGconstructsquery-specificmultimodal
graphs, matches query relations to semantic paths, and uses graph structure to guide the reuse
andrecomputationofcachedmodelstates.
Experiments across table annotation, dynamic classification, textual generation, and multi-
modalquestionansweringshowimprovedeffectivenessoverestablishedbaselinesandeﬀiciency
gainsingraphindexingandcontextprocessing. Ablationstudiesverifythecontributionsofthe
structural components. A related study of hyper-relational knowledge graphs examines infor-
mation preservation. These studies demonstrate how explicit relationships improve evidence
representation,retrieval,andcomputationforlanguagemodels.
