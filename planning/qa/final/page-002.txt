Structural Augmented Reasoning for Language
Models
byYuboWang
DepartmentofComputerScienceandEngineering
TheHongKongUniversityofScienceandTechnology
Abstract
Language models depend on the evidence supplied to their prediction and generation pro-
cesses. Tables can omit information needed for semantic annotation, classification labels can
evolve under limited supervision, and answers can require relationships distributed across tex-
tualandvisualsources. Thisthesisinvestigateshowexplicitknowledgestructurescanorganize
andselectevidenceforthesesettings.
The thesis develops four methods. KGLink combines knowledge-graph information with a
pre-trainedlanguagemodelforcolumntypeannotation. Cross-columngraphfiltering,candidate
types, and a contextual representation objective relate external knowledge to the target annota-
tion vocabulary. GORAG maintains weighted keyword–label relations for dynamic few-shot
classification. Its connected retrieval and online indexing adapt candidate evidence to the input
and the information available across rounds. AGRAG selects connected textual evidence using
query-conditioned node influence and semantic edge costs, then combines the selected graph
with hybrid passage retrieval. MGRAG incrementally constructs query-specific multimodal
graphs, matches query relations to semantic paths, and uses graph features to guide selective
recomputationofcachedmodelstates.
EvaluationscoverannotationonSemTabandVizNet,dynamicclassificationonCAD,WOS
and IFS-Rel, textual retrieval and generation, and multimodal question answering. KGLink
obtains weighted F1 of 96.07% on VizNet. GORAG reaches fourth-round WOS accuracy of
0.4929 with ten examples per label and Qwen2.5-7B. AGRAG obtains Medical summarization
coverage of 0.690 with Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct. MGRAG reaches MMQA accuracy of 0.7442
with Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct. Ablations examine knowledge enrichment, index maintenance,
connected context, semantic matching, and context recomputation. A separate hyper-relational
knowledge-graph study analyzes information preservation. The studies identify how explicit
relationshipsguideevidencerepresentation,retrieval,andcomputationforlanguagemodels.
