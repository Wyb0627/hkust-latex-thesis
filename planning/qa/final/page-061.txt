5.1 Research Problem and Evidence Requirements
A question can require facts from several parts of a corpus. Independently retrieved passages
may repeat one fact or leave the relationships between different facts implicit. A graph records
these relationships, but retrieval must still select a subgraph that contains enough information
fortheanswer.
We propose AGRAG to select connected evidence using query relevance and graph influ-
ence. Statistical extraction determines the entity vocabulary, semantic costs rank connections,
and neighborhood expansion adds facts around the query anchors. A hybrid passage channel
suppliescontextomittedbythegraphrepresentation.
Consider a corpus containing statements about an illness, the organ that it affects, and the
routesthroughwhichitspreads. Aquerymaymatchfactsmentioningtheillnessandtheorgan.
Connecting those facts can identify a small initial structure, but answering the question may
require a neighboring fact that specifies another anatomical component. The original example
in Figure 5.2 illustrates this distinction. The example motivates expansion beyond an initial
terminal-connectingstructure.
